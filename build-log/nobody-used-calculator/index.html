The Correct Calculator Nobody Used

By the time I reach the department in the morning, the day’s FDG is already waiting. I load it into the dispenser, enter the production time and the dose, run QC, and the room is ready before the first patient arrives. Same sequence, every morning, at the hour when attention is thinnest.

The dose calculation itself is not hard. That is the trap. Because it is easy, it never gets prioritized for a tool; because it is repeated under time pressure, it is exactly where a slip would live. For years I did it the way everyone did it — by hand, in my head, in the order the textbook teaches.

So I built a calculator. Web-based, no budget, no committee, no project code, put together with an AI coding assistant in the cracks of the week. I shaped it around the one thing I knew that a textbook doesn’t: the order information actually arrives on exam day. The first screen doesn’t ask for the formula. It asks for the three things the delivery tells you when it lands: activity, production time, expiration time, in that order, because that is the order the morning hands them to you.

It worked. It was correct. And almost nobody used it but me.

People work differently. Everyone had built their own way of getting through the morning, and a tool — even a right one — asks them to set that way down and pick up a new one. I couldn’t make them. And the moment I imagined making them, I understood why I shouldn’t: forcing the change would cost the only thing that makes a change survive, which is a person willing to carry it. Push hard enough and you don’t get adoption. You get someone spending their attention resisting you instead of using the tool.

I have now been on both sides of this. Two decades of being handed tools built for a spec instead of for my morning, and the one time I built the tool myself and watched it sit there, correct and unused. The model was never the risk. The willingness was.

Clinical AI doesn’t fail in the model. It fails at the bedside — not because the bedside is stubborn, but because that is where the willingness has to come from, and nobody builds for that.

I still open my calculator every morning. It is right about the dose.

한국어 요약

새벽, 출근하면 그날 FDG가 이미 와 있다. 디스펜서에 장착하고 생성시간·용량을 입력하고 QC를 마치면 첫 환자 준비가 끝난다. 선량 계산은 어렵지 않다 — 그래서 아무도 도구화하지 않았고, 나는 웹 계산기를 만들었다. 첫 화면은 교과서 공식이 아니라 약이 도착하며 알려주는 순서(용량·생성시각·유효시각)를 묻는다. 정확했다. 그런데 거의 나만 썼다. 사람마다 일하는 방식이 다르고, 아무리 옳은 도구도 그 방식을 내려놓으라 요구한다. 강제할 수 없고, 강제하면 변화를 이고 갈 사람의 시간과 원동력을 잃어 도태된다. 임상 AI가 병상에서 실패하는 건 병상이 완고해서가 아니라, 변화를 수용할 의지가 거기서 나와야 하는데 아무도 그걸 위해 설계하지 않기 때문이다.
